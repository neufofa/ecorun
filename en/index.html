01 / About

Who I am

Born in Reunion Island in 1995, I have built my entire path (academic, professional and voluntary) around the island and marine biodiversity of the Indian Ocean.

My training at the University of Reunion Island, completed by an exchange year at Université Laval in Quebec, gave me a dual competence: the conservation issues specific to the Mascarene islands (native, endemic and introduced species, and their management) and tropical marine ecosystems (coral reefs, mangroves, seagrass beds, fisheries resources).

My doctorate, defended in 2022, focused on the life-history traits and habitat selection of large coastal sharks around Reunion Island. I combined sclerochronology, stable isotopes (carbon, nitrogen, oxygen) and trace elements by laser ICP-MS to reconstruct their growth, their diet and their movements.

I then devoted two years to the conservation of the Reunion cuckooshrike (Coracina newtoni), one of the rarest birds in the world, found only on the Roche Écrite massif. Exceptionally demanding fieldwork: dense tropical forest, steep ramparts, weekly bivouacs and the supervision of hundreds of volunteers.

In 2024 and 2025 I served as environment officer for the TAAF (French Southern and Antarctic Lands) in the Scattered Islands, mainly on Juan de Nova. As the only scientist on a remote island, sharing it with a military detachment, I carried out all the species and ecosystem monitoring used to identify conservation priorities.

In 2026 I took part in the Barau's petrel (Pterodroma baraui) rescue campaign with SEOR: collecting grounded fledglings across the whole island, treating them and returning them to flight, to counter the effects of light pollution.

Since 2026, ECORUN has served organisations that need high-level scientific support without being able to recruit full time: research, conservation, fieldwork, analysis, writing and outreach.
